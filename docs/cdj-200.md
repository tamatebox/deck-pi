# CDJ-200 donor boards

Three boards out of a Pioneer **CDJ-200**: the pitch fader (`SLDB`), the jog
(`JOGB`), and the play/cue switch panel (`KSWB`). They are objects on the bench
rather than parts in a catalogue, which is why this file exists at all —
`decisions.md` forbids recording an inventory precisely because a list of owned
parts goes stale, and exempts the case this file is: **a requirement becomes a
check to run against a real object, and what comes back is a measurement.**

Everything below is either transcribed from the connectors or measured. Where it
is neither, it says so.

**These boards carry no behaviour, and nothing here revises `controls.md`.** A
switch out of a 200 closing a contact says nothing about what the deck does when
it closes; what a control *means* is the deck's own decision and lives in
`controls.md`. This paragraph used to answer a narrower question — whether a
CDJ-350 paper reference conflicted with donor boards out of a 200 — and that
question cannot arise now, because **there is no reference player at all.** The
350 was removed from these documents on 2026-09-21 and `decisions.md` has the
ruling. No CDJ-200 documentation is cited anywhere in this tree either.

## The connectors

Transcribed from the boards, supplied by the user. Pin 1 first.

**`SLDB` — pitch fader, `CN603`**

| | Signal | To the Pico |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | GNDF | GND |
| 2 | GNDAD | AGND (physical 33, the only one) |
| 3 | ADIN | GP27_A1 |
| 4 | ADCT | not connected |
| 5 | V5AD | 3V3 |

**`JOGB` — jog, `CN604`**

| | Signal | To the Pico |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | GNDD | GND |
| 2 | JOG1 | undecided |
| 3 | JOG2 | undecided |
| 4 | V+5V | 3V3 |

**`KSWB` — play and cue, `CN602`**

| | Signal | To the Pico |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | GLED | undecided |
| 2 | INDL | undecided |
| 3 | V+5V | 3V3 |
| 4 | CUEL | LED, dropped |
| 5 | PLAYL | LED, dropped |
| 6 | PLAY | GP4 |
| 7 | CUE | GP5 |
| 8 | KD2 | GP26_A0, with the pad's pull-up — see below |
| 9 | GNDA | GND |

**The silk says `V+5V` and 5 V is not what goes in.** All three boards run at
**3.3 V**, which is also what makes the fader's divider ratiometric against the
RP2350's converter with nothing in between. The Pico has **one** 3V3 pin, so
three boards at once need it distributed; GND is available several times over.

**`KD2` carries six buttons on one wire.** Of the nine pins, three are button
signals: PLAY and CUE have their own lines, and **the remaining six share
`KD2`** — SEARCH x2, TRACK SEARCH x2, FOLDER SEARCH x2. Supplied by the user
from the board.

**It is a resistor ladder, and this is now measured rather than inferred.** The
board carries `R605`, `R606` and `R610`-`R613` — six resistors for six buttons —
and each press does present its own level on `KD2`. Six distinct plateaus, well
separated, are below. The part numbers are the user's; the levels are this
file's.

Two consequences follow from the ladder before any value is measured:

- **The pull-up has to come from the deck.** Six resistors is one per button and
  no divider top. The measurement below used the RP2350 pad's own pull-up, which
  is weak, high-impedance and loosely specified — enough to tell six buttons
  apart and **not** enough to fix a threshold against.
- **A ladder cannot report two buttons at once**, and *what* it reports instead
  is **not measured**. Six resistors serve six buttons either way, so the part
  count does not say which topology this is, and the two differ exactly here: a
  **series-tapped** chain shorts the node to the lower of the two taps, so the
  deck sees one of the six known levels; **six resistors in parallel to ground**
  present their parallel combination, which is **below both** and need not land
  near any measured level. The sentence that stood here — "whichever press pulls
  hardest wins" — is only true of the first, and was written before either was
  checked. Nothing in `controls.md` asks for a chord, so nothing depends on the
  answer today; what would be wrong is a future reader taking it as measured.
  **One press of any two buttons at once settles it — at the raw level**,
  against the levels below: landing on one of them is series, landing below both
  is parallel. If it is parallel, `Ladder::poll`'s N-sample confirmation does not
  help — it rejects transients, and a held chord is not a transient.

  **Through HID, one chord has been pressed** (2026-10-22, on `phono`): SEARCH
  ◀◀ with TRACK ◀◀ arrives as **TRACK ◀◀ alone**, held, with no stray key either
  side of it. That is the deck's behaviour for that pair, and it **does not
  settle the topology**: series lands on TRACK ◀◀'s level, and parallel would land
  near 1590 by the levels' own arithmetic — estimated — which is TRACK ◀◀'s bucket
  too. The raw count through `faderprobe` is what tells the two apart.

## What is wired today

**`this-build.md` says**, and is the only document that does — clips or solder,
and which board is connected at all.

**The firmware followed the wire, not the other way round.** PLAY and CUE were
clipped to GP4 and GP5, so `main.rs` now reads them there and ENTER moved to
GP7, which PLAY vacated; GP8 is free. That is a firmware assignment and not a
fact about the Pi — `hardware.md` says why the distinction matters. The browse
encoder still holds GP2 and GP3 and is still what the jog would collide with.

## The fader, measured

Measured **2026-09-18** on the deck's own Pi (`deck-pi`, `6.18.50+rpt-rpi-v8`)
with a Pico 2 H running `firmware` built `--features faderprobe`, reading GP27
through the RP2350's converter at 12 bits, **single-shot at 10 Hz with no
oversampling and no averaging**.

| | counts | of travel |
|---|---|---|
| Bottom stop | **0** | 0% |
| Centre detent | **2048** | 50.0% |
| Top stop | **4095** | 100% |
| Span | **4095** | the converter's full scale |

The detent figure is the mean of seven passes through it (2055, 2038, 2051,
2041, 2050, 2051, 2053). **The detent is the electrical midpoint**, 2047.5,
inside a count — so unity may be taken from the arithmetic centre, and a
calibration step is a convenience rather than a correctness requirement.

The detent is also visible in the sweep itself: crossing it, consecutive samples
close to 17 counts apart where the rest of the stroke moves ~100 per sample,
because the notch slows the finger. It appears at the same place on all six
traversals.

**Noise**, hands off at the detent, 322 samples over 32 s:

| | counts | pitch |
|---|---|---|
| peak to peak | 55 | **0.27%** |
| 1st–99th percentile | 27 | 0.13% |
| 1 sigma | 7.78 | 0.038% |

Pitch figures assume the full stroke is ±10%, which makes **one count 0.0049%**
— the "ample by arithmetic" claim in `hardware.md`, confirmed. **It is not the
number that matters.** Resolution is 8x finer than the noise, so the fader is
noise-limited, not quantisation-limited, and

- **This lands on the pitch value itself, not on a UNITY gate.** The first
  reading of these figures sized a "near centre" deadband from them, which the
  design has already refused: *"Unity is a button, not a deadband on the fader"*
  — `architecture.md`, which rejects inferring the mode from an analog value
  every block precisely to avoid threshold width, hysteresis and flip-flopping.
  **`hardware.md`'s line about the detent making that gate "a physical fact" is
  the one out of step**, and is not resolved here.

  What the noise does bound is what v2 feeds libsoxr. **0.27% peak to peak
  arrives as pitch wobble**, on a stationary fader nobody is touching, so
  smoothing is not a refinement — it is the difference between a fader that
  holds a pitch and one that does not.
- **Effective resolution is about 7.3 bits, not 12** (1 sigma of 7.78 counts
  against an ideal 0.289). This is the *system* — pot, wiring, 3V3 off the Pi's
  USB — and not the converter's own ENOB, which is still unmeasured and not
  separable from this figure.
- Oversampling is the obvious lever and is untried: 16 averaged samples would
  put 1 sigma near 1.9 counts if the noise is white, which has **not** been
  established — 32 s of 10 Hz samples contains drift as well as noise.

### Not measured

- **Dead band at either stop.** Both ends sit exactly on a rail, and every sweep
  paused at the stop, so a stretch of travel that produces no change cannot be
  told from a stretch of time. A sweep that reverses without pausing would
  settle it.
- **Linearity**, beyond the gross shape of a hand sweep, and **monotonicity** —
  the sample rate is far below what a missing-code check needs.
- Temperature and long-term drift.

### How the measurement is repeated

`cat /dev/ttyACM0` **does not work and fails silently** — it blocks with no
output and no error. The probe's CDC interface needs `CLOCAL` set *and* DTR
raised, and `stty` cannot do it: its settings are gone by the time the next
process opens the port. Install `picocom` on the Pi and use that. There is no
`picocom`, `socat`, `screen` or `minicom` on `deck-pi` as of this measurement.

**On a Mac, `screen` reads it** — `screen /dev/cu.usbmodemdeck_pico_12 115200`.
`screen -dmS probe -L <port> 115200` logs to `screenlog.0` in
the working directory, which is how the 2026-10-22 numbers below were taken
without a terminal held open. Since that day each line also ends `play=` and
`cue=`, the debounced state of the two digital buttons, so the whole switch panel
can be checked with no Linux host.

**What the deck receives is read on the Pi, and needs no `evtest`.** The user is in
`input`, so the Consumer Control node reads directly:
`stdbuf -o0 od -An -v -w24 -t u2 /dev/input/eventN` prints one `input_event` per
line on a 64-bit kernel — field 9 the type, 10 the code, 11 the value — and
`EV_KEY` is type 1. `/sys/class/input/eventN/device/capabilities/key` is the
declaration, as hex words highest first. Both were how the 2026-10-22 run below
was taken.

## The switch panel, measured

Same rig and same day as the fader. PLAY and CUE are digital, `KD2` is the
ladder, sampled **single-shot at 20 Hz** — 10 Hz was raised because a press is
short enough that the slower cadence can miss one whole, which on a ladder reads
as a dead button rather than as a missed sample.

### PLAY and CUE work, and CUE's usage is confirmed on hardware

`evtest` on the **Consumer Control** node:

| Press | Scan | Keycode |
|---|---|---|
| PLAY | `c00cd` | `KEY_PLAYPAUSE` (164) |
| CUE | `c0226` | **`KEY_STOP` (128)** |

`main.rs` warns that the obvious usage for CUE — Consumer `0x0b7`, named "Stop"
— maps to `KEY_STOPCD` (166), which `src/input.rs` does not match, and that AC
Stop `0x226` is needed instead. **That warning had never been tested against a
real device and is now confirmed.**

One press produced exactly one press event and one release, with nothing
spurious, so **the 40 ms debounce is enough for these switches.** No bounce was
observed at all; this is not a measurement of the bounce, only of its absence
over a few presses.

### The ladder's six levels

Idle sits near **3950** — not at the rail, so something on the board loads the
line. Medians and the middle half of each press, from the run where all six
registered:

| Button | Median | Middle 50% | Gap to the next |
|---|---|---|---|
| *idle* | ~3950 | | 889 |
| SEARCH ◀◀ | **3061** | 3039–3082 | 613 |
| SEARCH ▶▶ | **2448** | 2419–2492 | 810 |
| TRACK ◀◀ | **1638** | 1607–1675 | 656 |
| TRACK ▶▶ | **982** | 946–1024 | 469 |
| FOLDER ◀◀ | **513** | 487–521 | 404 |
| FOLDER ▶▶ | **109** | 90–115 | — |

The order is the press order, and it was confirmed afterwards by pressing
FOLDER ▶▶ alone and getting the bottom level back.

**These are not thresholds and should not be turned into any.** Across three
runs the same button moved by up to 80 counts, which is larger than the spread
within a single run, and **FOLDER ▶▶ went 154 → 109 → 29 monotonically** — a
trend rather than noise, most likely contact resistance settling. Every level
will have to be taken again once the pad's pull-up is replaced by an external
resistor of known value, which is the change that makes a threshold worth
writing down.

Jumper wires in place of the clips, the same pad pull-up, the same 20 Hz

seconds. The press order was not the one asked for, so the levels were matched to
the table above, and SEARCH ◀◀ was then pressed alone to pin the matching down: it
read **3068**.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| *idle* | ~3950 | **3952** | 3941–3961 | — |
| SEARCH ◀◀ | 3061 | **3055** | 3043–3067 | −6 |
| SEARCH ▶▶ | 2448 | **2669** | 2631–2697 | **+221** |
| TRACK ◀◀ | 1638 | **1805** | 1782–1825 | **+167** |

**Every plateau stayed inside its own bucket, and SEARCH ▶▶ only just.** Its

a boundary set as the midpoint of a median that has since moved 221. One run is
not enough to say how often it would cross, so **the boundary moved to 2862** the
same day — midway between this run's two SEARCH medians, which leaves about 140
counts below it and 170 above. The other five boundaries kept their margins and
did not move.

### Through the host, 2026-10-22

`phono` (`6.18.39+rpt-rpi-v8`), the control path with the 2862 boundary and FOLDER
sent, read off the Consumer Control node as *How the measurement is repeated*
says. The kernel declared exactly ten keycodes — 28, 128, 158, 163, 164, 165,
168, 208, 402, 403 — and one press of each of the panel's eight buttons arrived as
its own keycode, one down and one up, with nothing else between them:

| Button | Scan | Keycode |
|---|---|---|
| PLAY | `c00cd` | 164 |
| CUE | `c0226` | 128 |
| SEARCH ◀◀ | `c00b4` | 168 |
| SEARCH ▶▶ | `c00b3` | 208 |
| TRACK ◀◀ | `c00b6` | 165 |
| TRACK ▶▶ | `c00b5` | 163 |
| FOLDER ◀◀ | `c009d` | 403 |
| FOLDER ▶▶ | `c009c` | 402 |

That is the whole chain for every button on this panel — level, bucket, bit,
usage, keycode — on the kernel the deck runs. One run, one press each.

**Two buttons moved and four did not**, which points at the switches rather than
the wiring: the jumpers are in series with every button, so a change there would
move all six. Contact resistance that depends on how a button is pressed fits,
and so does FOLDER ▶▶'s drift above — **inferred, not measured.** If that is
the cause, the external pull-up does not cure it: that resistor's case is the
pad's loosely specified value, not the switches. What cures it is boundaries with
room for a level to move.

### One press already lands in another button's bucket

**Pressing TRACK ▶▶ produced a sample of 3029 on the way, and SEARCH ◀◀ sits at
3061.** Not a hypothetical: it is in the recorded trace, from a deliberate
press of a different button.

So the ladder cannot be read the way `Debounced` reads a pin. Edge debounce has
nothing to work with here — the line passes *through* valid levels on the way to
its own. What it needs instead is **the same bucket, every tick, for
`DEBOUNCE_TICKS`**, which is the count that already debounces a switch and
comfortably outlasts a transition. `Ladder` in `firmware/src/main.rs` is that.

### What each level means

Boundaries are the midpoints of the 2026-09-18 medians, **except SEARCH's**: that
one moved from 2755 to **2862**, 2026-10-22's midpoint, when SEARCH ▶▶ came within
35 counts of it. A failed conversion holds
the settled bucket rather than releasing it: a dropped reading is not a released
button, and releasing one would end a seek the operator is still holding.

| Button | Median | Above | Emits |
|---|---|---|---|
| *idle* | ~3950 | 3506 | nothing |
| SEARCH ◀◀ | 3061 | **2862** | `KEY_REWIND` |
| SEARCH ▶▶ | 2448 | 2043 | `KEY_FASTFORWARD` |
| TRACK ◀◀ | 1638 | 1310 | `KEY_PREVIOUSSONG` |
| TRACK ▶▶ | 982 | 748 | `KEY_NEXTSONG` |
| FOLDER ◀◀ | 513 | 311 | `KEY_CHANNELDOWN`, **ignored by the deck** |
| FOLDER ▶▶ | 109 | — | `KEY_CHANNELUP` — **ENTER for now**, `this-build.md` |

**The two FOLDER levels are sent, and the deck ignores them.** Until 2026-10-22
they were decoded and dropped, to keep them out of the idle bucket where a
working button would be indistinguishable from a broken wire. Dropping them did
not achieve that: past the Pico, a button that emits nothing looks the same as a
dead one. Sent, a press shows in `evtest` — `decisions.md` has the reversal.
What they should *do* is open: the CDJ-200 walks a flat numbered
list of folders with wrap-around, skipping any with no playable tracks, while
`src/browser.rs` walks a tree. The two are views of the same data and can
coexist; the ordering that flattens the tree is not chosen.

**SEARCH is OR'd with GP9/GP10**, not replacing them. A discrete switch and the
panel both mean the same control, and either may be the one that is wired.
**TRACK has no pins of its own** — the ladder is its only source. This sentence
used to pair TRACK with GP4/GP5, which are PLAY and CUE.

The TRACK pair is what ended `controls.md`'s compression of four CDJ buttons
into two — `decisions.md` has the reversal. Its usages were read out of
`hid-input.c` on `rpi-6.18.y` and the numbers off the running image's
`input-event-codes.h`, because the CUE trap is the same trap twice.

## What this does not settle

The Pico's pin allocation. Three things are open and none is a fact about these
boards:

- **The jog collides with the browse encoder**, which holds GP2 and GP3, and the
  two are separate controls — `hardware.md` wants a detented encoder for the
  menu and a non-detented optical one for the platter.
- **The jog cannot use the browse encoder's decoder.** `main.rs` samples every
  input on a 1 ms tick; `hardware.md`'s 100-200 PPR at x4 puts a scrub near one
  count per 0.6 ms (**estimated**, from the PPR figure and an assumed two
  revolutions per second). PIO is the usual answer on an RP2350, and a PIO
  quadrature program wants its two pins adjacent and in order.
